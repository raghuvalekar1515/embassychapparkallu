What the Embassy Chapparkallu cost sheet lists

A cost sheet is the priced breakdown of one home, and it is the document to compare, not a headline figure. For a home at Embassy Origins, expect it to show:

  • the home itself: the address (Embassy Riverine or Embassy South Reserve), the configuration, the tower or villa corridor, and the area it is priced on;
  • the base price of that home;
  • car parking, if it is priced separately;
  • clubhouse charges, if any, for the Apartment Clubhouse or the Villa Clubhouse, whichever serves your address;
  • maintenance: any advance or deposit collected before handover;
  • taxes, shown as separate lines;
  • stamp duty and registration, which are extra and may be shown as an estimate.

If a charge you expected is missing, ask whether it is included or simply not yet listed. Keep a dated copy of every cost sheet you are given.

Comparing Eco Apartment and Villa Home prices

Because the two collections differ so much in size, compare like with like. Divide each quoted price by the same measure of area, and check that both cost sheets use the same measure, carpet or super built-up. The Floor Plans page explains the difference.

For a Villa Home, also ask what the price covers outside the walls: the garden that the living, dining and kitchen open onto, and any land that comes with the villa. For an Eco Apartment, ask whether the price changes with the floor, the tower or the view towards Tarahunise Lake.

How a construction-linked payment plan works

Payment plans for Embassy Chapparkallu are shared with the price list. On a new launch, the usual form is a construction-linked plan: a booking amount, then instalments that fall due as the building reaches set stages, and a final payment at handover.

For an Eco Apartment the stages usually follow the tower: the foundation, the floor slabs as they rise, then the finishing work. For a Villa Home they follow the villa’s own structure and finishes. The plan you receive lists each stage and the share of the price due at it, so you can see when money leaves your account.

Ask how you will be told that a stage is complete, and how long you have to pay once a demand is raised.

Home loans for Embassy Chapparkallu

Most buyers fund part of the price with a home loan. The lender decides how much it will lend from your income, your existing loans and its own review of the project and the home. Your own money covers the rest, including the booking amount, which is usually paid before any loan is disbursed.

With a construction-linked plan, the loan is paid out in parts as each demand is raised, not in one go. Until the full loan is disbursed, many lenders charge interest only on the amount paid out so far, often called pre-EMI, and full EMIs start later. Ask your lender which option it offers for a home of the size you are choosing, whether a Studio or a 5 BHK Villa Home.

What to check before paying a token for Embassy Chapparkallu

A token or booking amount holds a home for you. Before you pay one, check:

  • the exact home it holds: address, configuration, tower or villa corridor, and area;
  • the price of that home on a dated cost sheet;
  • the payment plan that will follow;
  • whether the token is refundable, and on what terms, if you decide not to go ahead;
  • that the receipt names the company selling the home and that the payment goes to that company’s account.
